01 / PSYCHODYNAMIC PSYCHOTHERAPY

Understanding what lies beneath the surface.

Psychodynamic psychotherapy creates space to explore the meanings, feelings and relationships that shape our experience, including those that are not immediately clear to us.

What is psychodynamic psychotherapy?

We are shaped by relationships, histories and experiences that may continue to influence us in ways we do not fully recognise. Psychodynamic psychotherapy invites reflection on these connections, rather than treating distress as something separate from the person experiencing it.

There is no expectation that you arrive knowing exactly what to say. What feels confusing, repetitive, painful or difficult to articulate can become part of the conversation.

The therapeutic relationship

Psychodynamic work also pays attention to the relationship between therapist and client. Feelings and patterns that emerge in this relationship can sometimes offer a way to understand experiences elsewhere in life.

It is a process of thinking together, with care and curiosity, rather than receiving ready-made explanations.

Reasons people seek therapy

People come to therapy for many reasons: difficulties in relationships, anxiety, loss, transitions, questions of identity or belonging, or a sense that something is troubling them without a clear name.

There is no single reason you need to have before making an enquiry.
